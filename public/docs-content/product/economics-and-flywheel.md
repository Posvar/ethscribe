# Economics and flywheel

Ethscribe is testing whether public research can create collectible artifacts and fund further preservation. The research must remain worth exploring even when no trade occurs.

## The current model

The marketplace charges 5% on sales settled through its contract. The seller receives the remaining 95% as claimable credit. The treasury claims its own fee balance.

This is not an enforced royalty on every future Ethscription sale. Transfers and trades elsewhere need not pay Ethscribe.

At a 5% rate, 10 ETH of marketplace sales produces 0.5 ETH of gross fee income before operating costs. That arithmetic says nothing about whether buyers will arrive or whether volume will recur.

## What would make the loop work

1. A well-scoped expedition attracts attention.
2. Hunters recover useful artifacts and document their sources.
3. Visitors return for discoveries and the developing historical record.
4. Some collectors purchase recognized Ethscriptions.
5. Fees help support the next expedition and ongoing maintenance.

The current release supports the first hunt and fixed-price trading. It does not automatically fund or launch another expedition when a sale occurs.

## Possible later rewards

A future treasury policy could allocate revenue to expedition authors, useful research, preservation, and operations. No author or researcher reward pool is implemented or owed under today's contract.

A separate distributor can become the recipient for future market fees without upgrading custody. Existing listings retain their captured recipient. Exact allocations, eligibility, and funding would need to be published before a reward program begins.

## Why no participation token

Rewarding page views, approvals, or wallet count is easy to game. The experiment first needs evidence of useful contributions: recovered sources, reproducible extractions, independent corroboration, and corrections that change a conclusion.

Wallet-signed history can make that work attributable without turning every action into a token payout.

## Measures that matter

- Independent participants who complete a Finding.
- Return visits and contributions across expeditions.
- Sources or corrections added to someone else's work.
- Purchases between independent collectors and sellers.
- Research and support time required per accepted artifact.
- Operating costs covered by repeat activity.

Gross trading volume, self-trades, or vote totals should not stand in for a healthy community. The first investment is making one expedition useful and enjoyable enough that people want another.
