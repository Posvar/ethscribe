# Expedition 000: EBURP — Before the Punks

**A completed collection, not a new hunt.**

[Explore the characters](/expeditions/eburp#eburp-collection) or [read the full story](/expeditions/eburp#eburp-story).

Before CryptoPunks, Matt Hall and John Watkinson were making little adventurers for little screens. Expedition 000 brings an existing collection of 216 Gurk-character Ethscriptions into Ethscribe, with their original PNG bytes, catalogue identities, creation records, and source evidence intact.

The story follows the early mobile game, the 2013 EBURP source release, and [@posvar's](https://x.com/posvar) 2023 preservation effort. These are earlier works in the creators' history—not CryptoPunks or a new, endorsed Larva Labs collection.

## Two parts of one record

| Collection section | What it contains | Marketplace treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Tradeable Core 92 | Nine heroes, 65 enemies and 18 non-player characters matched to the public EBURP repository | An eligible current owner can deposit an existing Ethscription and list it |
| Burned Archive 124 | Characters outside that open-source subset, preserved as Ethscriptions and subsequently sent to the Ethereum burn address | Historical records only; no deposit or trading controls |

All 216 are native 16 × 16 PNG files. The gallery keeps their original images and metadata; enlarging the display does not change the underlying file.

**Complete** means the existing catalogue contains 216 Ethscription IDs. It does not mean every artifact is currently for sale, held by Ethscribe, or freshly rechecked by an indexer. The archive's historical burn account is separate from any live ownership observation.

## If you already own a core character

There is no re-ethscribing step and no new Finding to publish.

1. Open your character in [Expedition 000](/expeditions/eburp) and connect its current owning wallet.
2. The record checks the original Ethscription ID, its recorded payload and current ownership before offering **Deposit into marketplace**.
3. Review and approve that Ethereum deposit. Wait for confirmation and custody verification.
4. Open [Field Wallet](/wallet) to set a price, manage a listing, or withdraw. If a position requires registration for sale, the wallet presents that separate step.

If the record shows an older EBURP vault or another escrow as owner, use that system's supported withdrawal flow to return the Ethscription to your wallet first. Ethscribe does not infer an old vault's beneficial depositor or automatically move assets out of it.

This uses Ethscribe's **existing immutable marketplace** and its existing 5% settlement fee. The original Ethscription ID and history remain unchanged. There is no replacement token, new contract, cross-chain move, or automatic transfer when the collection appears on this site. Old marketplace listings do not become new listings automatically. Depositing, listing and selling remain separate actions. [Ownership and marketplace](../product/ownership-and-marketplace.md) explains the flow and claimable proceeds.

## What has been verified

The [byte audit](https://github.com/Posvar/ethscribe/blob/main/research/expedition-000/README.md) preserves the evidence boundaries:

- All 216 catalogue entries have unique IDs, unique raw-file hashes, and complete native PNG payloads.
- All 92 core files match pinned source downloads and the repository's first commit, dated 1 November 2013. This does not claim that every PNG is unchanged from the original 2005 handset game.
- The initial official-index audit fully matched 62 core creation records before rate limiting. The other 154 were not freshly verified in that pass; unchecked does not mean missing or invalid.
- The 124 archive entries have no original source URLs in the collection CSV. Their publisher filenames and a blanket reuse license are not invented.

Current ownership and sale availability are checked separately when a record opens. Historical snapshots never authorize a transaction.

## Sources and rights

The [first EBURP source commit](https://github.com/pents90/eburp/commit/dca3cf9a56c955ad87063253d8a34eaa38fcc002) and its [README](https://github.com/pents90/eburp/blob/dca3cf9a56c955ad87063253d8a34eaa38fcc002/README.md) connect the core files to Gurk III's engine. The README's reuse wording concerns included project material and draws a boundary around other Gurk art and music; it is not a blanket permission covering the archive.

Owning an Ethscription does not transfer copyright, the Gurk name, or the creators' endorsement. Primary historical references are linked beside each chapter in [the full story](/expeditions/eburp#eburp-story).
